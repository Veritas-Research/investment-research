# Research Notes

This is where I think out loud. Some notes are market commentary on whole sectors, based on what I am reading. Others explore a concept I keep running into, properly, with the data and the method laid out so you can check my work. Neither is a formal call on a single name.

## Two kinds of note

**Market notes.** Short to medium reads on a market, a sector, or an idea. The trigger is usually a book, a paper, an article, or a chart that reframes how I see something. I work through what it means, where the consensus is wrong or lazy, and where that points for capital. Each note states a stance and the risk to that stance, but the unit of analysis is the market, not the ticker.

**Concept explorations.** Deeper dives into how something actually works when the headlines use a word loosely. Example: when people say Chinese AI models are "cheaper", do they mean cheaper to buy, less electricity, or less computing power? These pieces show every source and the method, say which numbers are measured and which are estimates, and often come as interactive pages where you can change the assumptions yourself.

## How this differs from /theses

Theses are formal, single name valuation and investment cases with a verdict (buy, pass, short) and the math to back it. Example: the SpaceX Initial Public Offering (IPO) note.

Research notes (this folder) are broader and more exploratory. They connect what I am reading to what I am seeing in the market, or take one idea apart to see how it works. They may name sectors and directions of travel, but they are commentary, not a position. A research note can later harden into a thesis once a specific name and a price are in view.

## What to expect in a note

- A one line summary and a clear stance
- The reading or data that prompted it
- Where I think the consensus is mispriced or misframed
- Where the broader market is heading if I am right
- An honest section on where the view could be wrong
- For concept explorations: the method, every source, and which figures are measured versus estimated
